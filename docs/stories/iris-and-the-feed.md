# Iris and the Feed

*A story for ten-, eleven-, and twelve-year-olds.*
*Book Fourteen of the Scipraxian Tales.*

---

## A note before the story

This is the kid-direct social media Tale — the cousin to *Mira and the Are-Self* on the social-media side. It is told for the kid the way Mira is told for the kid, which means a grownup can read it out loud or hand it over. It is short. It is one evening and one morning long. It is built around three of the Twelve Variables: **Inclusion · Humility · Inquiry** — the three Mira's storybook starts with.

A scipraxian-shaped storybook is not a warning. It is a way of paying attention. *Iris and the Feed* is the way Iris learned to pay attention to the feed instead of being paid attention to *by* the feed. That is the whole story. The rest is what happened on the night she figured it out.

---

## Chapter 1 — The Sleepover

Iris was eleven the night she figured out the feed.

She was at Lacey's house. Lacey was twelve. Lacey lived in the green house at the end of Persimmon Street, the one with the porch swing that squeaked. Iris and Lacey had been best friends since kindergarten. They had been having sleepovers at Lacey's house since they were six. The sleepovers had a pattern: pizza for dinner, a movie, snacks in Lacey's room, talking until they fell asleep. The pattern had not changed in five years. The phones in their hands had.

It was Friday night. The pizza was eaten. The movie was over. They were in Lacey's room in their sleeping bags on the floor. Lacey's room had glow-in-the-dark stars on the ceiling that Lacey had stuck up when she was eight and that Lacey was now too old for and that Lacey's mom said she was not going to take down because *they make me happy when I tuck you in.*

Iris had her phone. Lacey had her phone. They were both on the same app. The app was the one with the videos. Most of the kids in fifth and sixth grade had it. Iris's mom had let her have it on her birthday, six months ago, with rules about how long she could be on it and what time she had to put it away.

Iris was scrolling. Lacey was scrolling. They had been scrolling for maybe ten minutes when Iris noticed something.

"Lacey."

"Yeah."

"What are you watching."

Lacey held her phone up. There was a video on it of a girl doing a dance Iris had never seen.

"I don't know who that is."

"What?"

"I have never seen that girl before."

Lacey looked at her phone.

"Iris. She has like four million followers. Everybody has seen her."

"I haven't."

Lacey sat up.

"Wait. Show me your screen."

Iris held her phone up. There was a video on it of a baby otter being fed by a person with a tiny bottle.

"I have never seen that otter," Lacey said.

"Lacey. The otter has like two million views."

They looked at each other.

"…hold on," Lacey said.

## Chapter 2 — The Cataloging

They sat up in their sleeping bags. They turned on the bedside lamp. Lacey got the notebook she used for school and a pencil. The notebook was the one with the cat sticker on the front that Iris had given her at Christmas.

"OK," Lacey said. "We're going to scroll. Both of us. Same time. We're going to write down what comes up on each of our screens. Then we're going to see how different it is."

"OK."

They scrolled together. They wrote.

After fifteen minutes, the page in the notebook looked like this:

> **IRIS**
> 1. Otter being fed
> 2. Pottery wheel making a bowl
> 3. Cake decoration with stars
> 4. Bird trying to open a jar
> 5. A girl singing in a car
> 6. Another otter
> 7. Person braiding hair
> 8. A goat in a sweater
> 9. Cake decoration with flowers
> 10. Another otter
>
> **LACEY**
> 1. Girl doing the dance
> 2. Different girl doing a different dance
> 3. Boy lip-syncing
> 4. Two girls doing a dance together
> 5. A skit about a cafeteria
> 6. Another dance
> 7. A girl crying about her boyfriend
> 8. A skit about a teacher
> 9. Boy doing a dance
> 10. A girl reviewing a lipstick

They looked at the list.

"Iris."

"Yeah."

"You don't have any people on your feed. Everything on your feed is *animals* and *cake.*"

"Lacey. You don't have any animals or cake. *Everything* on your feed is people."

They looked at each other.

"Why are they different."

Iris thought about it.

"…Because we watched different things."

"What."

"I think — Lacey, I think the app is showing us the things it thinks we will keep watching. And it knows what we will keep watching by what we watched before. So you watched a dance one time and now it shows you dances. And I watched an otter one time and now it shows me otters."

"Iris. That means I am never going to see otters."

"And I am never going to see the dance."

Lacey was very quiet for a moment. Then she said: "Iris. That means we are not seeing the same world."

---

## Chapter 3 — The Experiment

They thought about this for a few minutes. They both put their phones down on the floor between the sleeping bags.

Iris said: "Lacey. I want to try something."

"What."

"Let's both watch the otter. The same otter. On both of our phones. All the way through. Then let's both like it. Then we'll see what comes up next."

"OK."

Iris pulled up the otter video on her phone. She showed Lacey how to find the same one. They both watched. They both liked it. They scrolled to the next one.

On Iris's phone: another otter.

On Lacey's phone: a girl doing a dance.

"It didn't work," Lacey said.

"It might take more than one. The app probably doesn't change fast."

"Iris. The app isn't *for* us, is it."

Iris looked at her.

"What do you mean."

"Like — I thought the app was for showing us videos. But the app isn't for *that.* The app is for keeping us watching. If the app showed us videos we didn't want to watch, we would put it down. So it shows us the videos we will keep watching. It is not showing us *the world.* It is showing us *us.* It is showing each of us a tiny version of the world that is shaped like *us.*"

Iris held very still. Lacey was twelve. Lacey had figured out a thing Iris had not figured out yet, even though they were watching the same screens at the same time.

She said: "Lacey. That means — that means people who watch different things become different people. Not because they are different people. Because the *app* is different for them."

"Iris. That is — that is sort of scary."

"…yeah."

"What do we do about it."

Iris thought.

"I don't know yet. I think we tell my mom in the morning. I think we tell *your* mom in the morning. I think we tell them both. They are the moms. They will know."

"My mom has the app too. She doesn't watch dances."

"What does she watch."

"…I don't know. I have never asked her. I have never even thought about asking her."

They looked at each other. The glow-in-the-dark stars on the ceiling were doing the thing they did when the lamp had been off for a few minutes, getting a little brighter as your eyes adjusted.

"Lacey."

"Yeah."

"Tomorrow at breakfast we ask both of them what's on their feeds. We make them show us. We compare."

"OK."

"And we keep the notebook. We are going to keep doing this. I want to know."

"OK Iris."

They turned the lamp off. They lay there in their sleeping bags. Iris said good night. Lacey said good night. Iris was the first one to fall asleep, but only by a minute.

---

## Chapter 4 — Saturday Morning

In the morning they came down to the kitchen at eight-fifteen. Lacey's mom was at the table with her coffee and her own phone. The pancakes were going on the griddle. The kitchen smelled like the pancake batter and the coffee and the way Lacey's house always smelled on Saturday mornings, which was like the house Iris wished her own house smelled like.

"Mom."

"Yeah honey."

"Iris and I figured something out last night about the app. We want to ask you something. We want to make you part of the experiment."

Lacey's mom put her coffee down.

"OK. What's the experiment."

Iris explained. She showed Lacey's mom the notebook. She showed the cataloging from last night. She showed the otters and the cakes and the dances and the lip-syncs and the goat in the sweater. She told Lacey's mom what they had figured out — that the app was not showing them the world, the app was showing them themselves.

Lacey's mom read the notebook. She read it twice.

She said: "Girls. Let me get my phone. We're going to add me to the experiment."

She got her phone. She opened the app. She scrolled. They wrote down what came up.

> **LACEY'S MOM**
> 1. Recipe for one-pot chicken
> 2. A garden tour
> 3. A woman explaining a car repair
> 4. A book recommendation
> 5. Another recipe
> 6. A clip from an old movie
> 7. A teacher explaining a math thing
> 8. A funny thing about being tired
> 9. Another garden
> 10. A recipe

The girls looked at the list.

Iris said: "She doesn't have any of our things."

Lacey said: "And she doesn't have my things."

Lacey's mom said: "Girls. I have been on this app for two years and I have never once thought to ask what was on yours. I assumed it was the same world I was in. I am sitting at this table right now realizing I have been wrong about the app for two years."

She went quiet for a minute. Then she said:

"Iris. Can I keep the notebook for a day. I want to take this to your mom this afternoon. I want to show her. I think she should see this. I think *all* the moms should see this."

Iris said: "Yes. You can keep the notebook."

Lacey said: "Mom. We figured it out. Iris and I."

"Yes baby. You did. You figured out something most of the grownups have not figured out yet. I am proud of you. I am especially proud of you, Iris, because I know your mom does not let you have the app for very long each day, and you saw the thing in the time you were given."

Iris smiled at her plate. The pancakes were ready. They were the kind with the chocolate chips. Lacey's mom put three on each plate.

The asking had become the asking-back. The ripple had begun, in the green house at the end of Persimmon Street on a Saturday morning at eight-thirty AM, between two best friends and the mom in the kitchen and the notebook with the cat sticker on the front, who would tell the other moms, who would tell other kids, who would tell other moms, who would compare feeds, who would notice the world they thought they were in was not the world the kid in the next room was in, until enough of them had noticed that the asking-back about the feed had started to shape what people typed back into it on purpose, instead of by accident.

Iris ate her pancakes. She was eleven. She knew she had figured something out. She did not know yet how big the thing she had figured out was.

She was going to know. She was going to keep asking. She had a notebook.

## A note after the story

For grownups reading this with kids:

The three Variables in this Tale are the same three the *Mira and the Are-Self* storybook starts with — Inclusion, Humility, Inquiry. They land here as the kid-shape of the social-media problem: Inclusion (whose feed are we comparing?), Humility (mine isn't the only one), and Inquiry (why are they different?).

The Tale doesn't tell the kid what to do. It tells the kid how to *notice.* Noticing is what the platform spends a lot of money trying to prevent. A kid with a notebook and a friend and a Saturday morning is a real counterforce.

The Tale is also short on purpose. The kid version of *Have You Talked to Your Kids About Social Media?* should be readable in fifteen minutes by the kid herself, then talked about over breakfast. If your kid hasn't asked their friend's feed what's on it, this Tale is a way to suggest that without making it homework.

If you want the grownup-side companion Tales: **[Book Five — *A Public Pool*](./talking-to-kids-about-social-media-public-pool)** is the parent-discovery shape. **[Book Six — *A School Board Meeting*](./talking-to-kids-about-social-media-school-board-meeting)** is the policy-room shape. **[Book Thirteen — *A Saturday Brunch*](./talking-to-kids-about-social-media-saturday-brunch)** is the divorced-dad-with-thirty-hours shape. The full **[Twelve Variables and Creed](../scipraxianism)** are at the philosophy page; **[Mira and the Are-Self](./storybook)** is the AI-side kid-direct story.

The asking is the tap on the shoulder. The story is what happens after you turn around.
